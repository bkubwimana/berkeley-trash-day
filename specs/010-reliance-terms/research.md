# Research

The Ninth Circuit's `Nguyen v. Barnes & Noble` decision explains that a Terms hyperlink
alone—even near an action button—may be insufficient without notice or an affirmative act
showing assent. `Keebaugh v. Warner Bros.` similarly describes conspicuous notice plus an
unambiguous user action as important to online assent under California law.

Berkeley's official street-sweeping guidance tells drivers to check posted signs and move
their car before sweeping to avoid a ticket. Its residential waste guidance directs
residents to call Berkeley Zero Waste for collection days and missed pickups.

The implementation therefore combines a conspicuous warning, a linked Terms page, and an
affirmative checkbox at the product's reliance actions. This is a risk-reduction design,
not a representation that the Terms are necessarily enforceable or a substitute for advice
from a qualified attorney.
